On 11 November, Alibaba finally announced Qwen 4 — after a week of a Zhuangzi poster with no model name, no date. But read the announcement carefully, and the only thing you can actually use today is one model: Qwen4-Max-Preview. Not a model family. Just Max, just a preview, window open 11 to 25 November — then the model is retired.

For me this is the strangest part. No weights to download. No model card. No license. No official benchmark table. No pay-as-you-go API. The only way in is the Token Plan — a monthly subscription from $6 to $43, with an `sk-sp-` key that can't be swapped with a regular key. Qwen built its reputation on openness — Qwen3, whose weights anyone could download — and what arrived now is the opposite: closed, and paid through a subscription.

And there is a side worth reading to the end. One release tracker lists neat numbers: a 1.2-trillion-parameter MoE, a score of 58 on the Artificial Analysis index, $1/$4 per million tokens, Apache 2.0 weights promised before 20 December. Neat, convincing — and, as far as I checked, appearing on not a single official Alibaba page. I'm not saying the numbers are definitely wrong. I'm saying: numbers without a source aren't information, they're gossip in neat formatting.

For anyone tempted to try it, three terms are easy to miss. The Token Plan is designed for interactive use in coding tools — automation scripts and app backends are out of scope, and out-of-scope use can end in a suspended subscription. Data is processed from the Singapore region. And a Personal plan can't be cancelled — only auto-renewal can be switched off — and the cost is non-refundable. Subscribing to try it for two weeks means buying a full month.

So my measure is simple: a good model is one I can hold — download it, run it, measure it myself. Qwen 4 can't be held yet. I'm staying on Qwen3 while I wait, and I've marked 25 November not as a release day, but as the day the preview dies. If Alibaba goes quiet after that, we'll know the answer.
